# Colpan & Morck (NBER Working Paper, 2021) Paper Analysis

## Paper Information
- **Title:** Business Groups: Panics, Runs, Organ Banks and Zombie Firms
- **Authors:** Asli M. Colpan (Kyoto University), Randall Morck (University of Alberta & NBER)
- **Source:** NBER Working Paper No. 29035 (July 2021)
- **JEL Classification:** F65, G01, G21, G23, N2
- **Type:** Conceptual/Theoretical Review Paper (Based on Historical Case Studies)

---

## 1) What is the Theoretical Background of This Paper?

This paper is grounded in **Agency Theory** and **Corporate Governance** literature, particularly applying Jensen & Meckling (1976)'s agency concept to the business group context. Regarding banking crises, it builds upon Diamond & Dybvig (1983)'s **Bank Run Theory** and Bernanke (1983)'s research on how financial crises affect the real economy. The paper synthesizes Johnson et al. (2000)'s **Tunneling** concept and Khanna & Yafeh (2007)'s literature on **Internal Markets** functions of business groups. It borrows Caballero et al. (2008)'s **Zombie Firms** concept to explain mechanisms through which bank-centered business groups sustain low-productivity firms. The concept of **"Organ Bank (機關銀行)"** proposed by Kato (1957) in Japanese economic history serves as a key analytical tool.

---

## 2) What Important Research Questions Does This Paper Answer?

This paper addresses the following key research questions:

### Main Research Questions
1. **What is the role of banks within business groups?**
   - Differences between banks that subordinate other affiliates vs. banks that are subordinated by affiliates

2. **How do business groups respond to economic crises?**
   - Response mechanisms to idiosyncratic shocks vs. systemic shocks

3. **What is an "Organ Bank" and what problems does it cause?**
   - Moral hazard problem of banks subordinated by other affiliates providing reckless lending

4. **How are "Zombie Firms" created?**
   - Adverse selection problem where low-risk, low-productivity firms survive when banks dominate the group

5. **What policy solutions exist for business group problems?**
   - Analysis of historical cases including U.S. New Deal, Japanese zaibatsu dissolution, Israel's 2013 reforms

## 3) How Were the Variables Measured and What is Their Theoretical Basis?

### Note: This Paper is Not an Empirical Study

This is a **conceptual/theoretical review paper** that does not conduct statistical analysis with measured variables. Instead, it explains theoretical frameworks through **Historical Case Studies**.

### Key Conceptual Variables/Constructs

| Concept | Definition | Theoretical Basis |
|---------|------------|-------------------|
| **Agency** | Where effective decision-making authority resides | Jensen & Meckling (1976) Agency Theory |
| **Subordinating Bank** | Group bank that dominates other affiliates | Bank at apex prioritizes creditor interests → enforces low-risk strategies |
| **Subordinated Bank (Organ Bank)** | Group bank subordinated by other affiliates | Kato (1957)'s organ bank concept; reckless related-party lending |
| **Tunneling** | Non-market-price resource transfers within group | Johnson et al. (2000); expropriation through related-party transactions |
| **Zombie Firms** | Firms surviving through low-risk, low-productivity | Caballero et al. (2008); adverse selection in bank-dominated groups |
| **Moral Hazard** | Reckless risk-taking due to bailout expectations | Allen et al. (2015); reckless lending by organ banks |
| **Adverse Selection** | Survival of unproductive firms/banks | Bruche & Llobet (2014); zombie firm problem |
| **Internal Markets** | Intra-group transfers of capital/goods/services | Khanna & Yafeh (2007); substitutes for external market failures |

### Historical Cases Used in Analysis

| Country/Era | Case | Key Lesson |
|-------------|------|------------|
| Japan 1920s | Suzuki Zaibatsu collapse | Entire group collapses when organ bank fails |
| Japan 1970s | Mazda (Toyo Kogyo) rescue | Sumitomo Bank-led restructuring |
| Japan 1990s~ | Zombie firms | Low-productivity firm survival in bank-dominated groups |
| U.S. 1930s | New Deal reforms | Pyramid group dissolution policy |
| Turkey 2000-2001 | Financial crisis | Related-party lending problems of organ banks |
| Korea 1997 | Daewoo Group collapse | Bailout denied when political connections lost |
| Israel 2013 | Concentration Reduction Law | Pyramid limited to 2 tiers, financial-nonfinancial separation |
| Canada/Australia | 1980-90s | Strengthened tunneling regulations led to natural group dissolution |

### Key Contributions of This Paper

1. **Integrated Framework**
   - Bipolar model of Organ Bank vs. Subordinating Bank
   - Prediction of group behavior based on agency location

2. **Identification of Two Core Problems**
   - **Organ Bank Problem:** Moral hazard → Reckless lending → Bank crisis → Government bailout
   - **Zombie Firm Problem:** Adverse selection → Low-risk, low-productivity firm survival → Hindered economic growth

3. **Historical Comparative Analysis**
   - Synthesis of cases from Japan, U.S., Turkey, Korea, Israel, and others
   - Analysis of success/failure factors in policy interventions

4. **Policy Implications**
   - Presents various options: group dissolution, bank-nonfinancial separation, tunneling regulations, bailout denial
   - Analysis of advantages, disadvantages, and applicability conditions for each policy

### Paper's Limitations

- Relies on historical cases without empirical analysis
- Unresolved trade-off between social utility of internal markets vs. distortion costs in developing countries
- Lacks systematic causal analysis of policy effects

## Summary of Key Concepts

### Two Extreme Models

| Category | Organ Bank | Subordinating Bank |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| **Agency Location** | Non-financial affiliates | Bank |
| **Bank's Role** | Sacrifices for other affiliates | Controls other affiliates |
| **Lending Behavior** | Reckless related-party lending | Prudent lending, risk suppression |
| **Core Problem** | Moral Hazard | Adverse Selection |
| **Outcome** | Bank crisis → Government bailout | Zombie firm survival |
| **Example** | 1920s Japan Suzuki | Post-war Japan Keiretsu |

### Policy Options

| Policy | Example | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|--------|---------|------------|---------------|
| Group Dissolution | U.S. New Deal, Japan occupation | Fundamental solution | Politically difficult |
| Bank-Nonfinancial Separation | Glass-Steagall | Prevents organ banks | Shadow banking workaround possible |
| Strengthened Tunneling Regulations | Canada, Australia | Induces gradual dissolution | Loses internal market functions |
| Bailout Denial | Israel | Strengthens market discipline | Systemic crisis risk |
